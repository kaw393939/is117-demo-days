#### What the brand presentation suggests

<details>
<summary>My look, character, and trust notes for the three sources</summary>

- **Calendly:** **Look:** Blue/purple gradients, a centered promise, and clear actions. **Character:** Magician is a possible reading of removing scheduling friction. **Trust/action:** Free access provides value; it does not prove a reciprocity effect. This is a workflow reference, not a tutor.
- **Acuity:** **Look:** Warm color, serif headline, and branded booking examples. **Character:** Creator/Caregiver is plausible. Color and mixed type alone do not establish Postmodernism. **Trust/action:** Displayed customer testimony suggests social proof; it does not independently validate a tutoring need.
- **Wyzant:** **Look:** Subject-specific tutor profiles and matching choices. **Character:** Sage is a possible competence-focused reading. **Trust/action:** Qualifications/reviews are authority/social-proof cues to inspect. We did not verify tutors, prices, or learning outcomes.

These are interpretations of the linked pages, not official archetypes or independently verified claims. A functional grid alone does not establish historical Modernism.

</details>

**My proposed niche:** A first-year student who can name a topic but is unsure how to ask for help. **Promise:** An approachable first request for beginner web-development guidance.

**Source → decision:** Wyzant shows subject-specific tutor choices; Calendly and Acuity show scheduling workflows. I chose a patient Sage voice and a preparation list. I have not connected each persona need to a specific observation or checked whether beginners prefer this narrower offer.

**Still uncertain:** The references do not prove tutoring demand, tutor competence, or that the preferred window is available.

#### My audience choice

We chose beginning web-development students because they can use a small set of topics. Wyzant adds a tutoring alternative to our scheduling-tool references, but I have not explained which observed difference matters most to a beginner. The sources do not establish demand for our narrower service.

**What the research does not prove:** Website observations do not establish demand, customer preferences, sales, or the quality of a competing product.
