# From evidence to a design decision

Start with one real question instead of a blank page. Use the examples here to make a choice in the project you are already working on. **This is practice, not a new hand-in.**

## T-shirt shop: can I tell whether it will fit?

Compare an everyday shop and a premium shop. Notice where each gives you measurements, what it calls a fit, and which claims come from the seller. A product page can tell you what the brand promises. It cannot prove that the shirt is better or that people will buy yours.

Try this chain:

1. **Source:** I opened the product page and looked for fit guidance.
2. **Observation:** I had to leave the size selector to find measurements.
3. **Decision:** Put the fit guide beside size selection.
4. **Check:** Ask a classmate to choose a size they would buy. Watch whether they find the measurements without help.

Want more observations? See [Plain White Tee Studio's research](https://github.com/kaw393939/plain-white-tee-studio/blob/main/docs/research/landscape.md) and [White Tee Field Notes](https://github.com/kaw393939/is117007-tshirt/blob/main/docs/competitive-landscape.md). ## Brand: audience and voice are different decisions

A **persona** describes what a person needs in a situation. An **archetype** helps you choose how the brand speaks. For example, a commuter might need readable measurements; a Sage voice might explain how to get a useful measurement. An archetype is a design lens, not a claim about a customer's personality.

To compare two layouts, keep the shirt, copy, and main action the same. Move just the size guide or change the order of page sections. If you change everything at once, you won't know what helped. See the [Brand Studio reference](https://github.com/kaw393939/is117-brand-studio/blob/main/docs/reference.md) for background. The [older design library](https://github.com/kaw393939/archetype_design_persusion) is reference material, not a new assignment.

## Portfolio: make your own work easy to find

Imagine an internship reviewer with one minute. Can they find what **you** built, a way to try it, and one check that passed? This is a hypothesis about a reader, not a claim about every recruiter.

Write one short case study with those three links. Ask a classmate to point to your contribution without coaching them. If they can find the project but not your work, fix that before polishing colors. See the instructor's [persona examples](https://github.com/kaw393939/is117005-portfolio/blob/main/docs/portfolio-personas.md) and [evidence notes](https://github.com/kaw393939/is117005-portfolio/blob/main/docs/portfolio-evidence.md).

## Career research: compare work, not job titles

Read two actual job descriptions. Write down one task, one required skill and one preferred skill for each. Record the employer link and when you opened it. If you can't confirm something, say so rather than guessing. Job titles can sound different while describing similar work, or sound alike while asking for different skills.

The [AI Career Atlas](https://github.com/kaw393939/ai-career-research) is a place to find examples; the employer's original posting is where you check what is current. Follow the [course activity](/career/explore) for your own private Canvas hand-in.
