# Use AI and explain your work

Use AI coding tools and specification-driven development to help you plan, build, debug, and revise. Follow the course's existing AI-use and attribution policies in Canvas.

## Show judgment

- Give the tool a clear, bounded task tied to your specification.
- Inspect changes and understand their relationship to the rest of the system.
- Run the application and check the intended behavior and relevant failure cases.
- Correct generated mistakes and update your documentation.
- Be able to explain the code path, data flow, design choices, and your own decisions.

## Keep a concise AI-use record

Record the tool, purpose, relevant task/spec reference, what you accepted or changed, and how you verified it. A few specific examples are more informative than an enormous prompt transcript. Attribute borrowed code, imagery, and text appropriately.

## AI in development vs. AI in the product

Using AI to build a traditional business service meets the project's process direction. An AI feature in the product is optional and should solve a customer problem. If present, define what it does, what it must not do, how you test it, and when a person reviews the result. Use synthetic classroom data and keep credentials out of client-side code and public Git history.

During the demo, explain one place AI helped and one place human judgment, debugging, or verification was necessary.
