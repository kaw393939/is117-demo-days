## 1. Research

### A small competitive landscape

Source pages checked November 2, 2026. Observations concern the public pages, not a completed purchase, tested service, or proof of customer demand. For a portfolio, these are design references rather than business competitors.

| Source | Observation | Implication for this project |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Brittany Chiang](https://brittanychiang.com/) | Role introduction, project/experience evidence, professional links | Make role and evidence easy to find. |
| [Josh W. Comeau](https://www.joshwcomeau.com/) | An article-led site that demonstrates teaching | Use a short explanation to show reasoning. |
| [Lee Robinson](https://leerob.com/) | A concise introduction and writing links | Keep the introduction brief. These are experienced practitioners, not student achievement benchmarks. |

**Two positions:** A — Evidence-led builder portfolio. B — Skills-list-led portfolio.

### Two persona hypotheses

| | Persona 1 | Persona 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Situation | A team lead assessing a student’s problem-solving and collaboration | A recruiter quickly checking minimum project and contact information |
| Goal / need | Understand one business decision and the student’s own implementation and QA work. | A concise summary, projects, résumé, and professional links |
| Uncertainty | Is there evidence behind the claims, including AI-assisted work? | Does this product focus on my task? |
| Evidence and assumption | The reference pages provide ways to inspect relevant information. We hypothesize this visitor needs it too. | A plausible alternative audience; not validated by competitor pages alone. |

**Choice:** Prioritize clear project reasoning while retaining easy access to résumé and profiles. A persona summarizes a useful need; arbitrary ages, names, salaries, and hobbies would not establish evidence.

## 2. Plan

### Two concise website specifications

| | Approach A | Approach B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information order | Evidence first: a short decision and its validation introduce the projects. | Introduction first: role direction and skills precede project evidence. |
| Shared required workflow | Introduction → two case studies → contribution → résumé/profile information | Same workflow and information, presented in another order |
| Design direction | Sage; clear, careful, approachable | Same honest brand and content; alternate hierarchy/composition |
| Tradeoff | The selected need receives early emphasis. | An alternate entry point may suit a different visitor. |

Both approaches use the stories and acceptance criteria below. Comparing them changes the presentation; it does not double the entire technical scope.

### User stories → features → acceptance criteria

| ID | User story | Essential feature | Acceptance check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| JORDAN-RIVERA-1 | As a team lead, I want a concise case study so I can assess the student’s reasoning. | Problem, alternatives, choice, feedback, and limitation in each case study. | A visitor can identify one decision and its reason in each project. |
| JORDAN-RIVERA-2 | As a reviewer, I want to see how AI output was checked so I can assess ownership. | A short AI-use and verification statement tied to a feature. | The statement describes a specific human check and does not claim unrun tests passed. |
| JORDAN-RIVERA-3 | As a recruiter, I want clear profile and résumé access so I can evaluate next steps. | Dedicated professional-presence section and résumé download. | Sample profile drafts explain which fields must be replaced with actual account links. |

**Data / technology:** HTML, CSS, JavaScript; project details and professional drafts are static content. **Deferred:** Automated skills scores, advanced dashboard, and unsupported FDE-readiness claims.
