# Read the submission, grade, and feedback

Each example now contains **completed student work**, the working product, and an instructor-style assessment. Compare two examples of the same project to see why their scores differ.

## What the grade means

For each milestone, the instructor model uses the [published rubric](/course/milestones#how-each-step-is-graded): **Complete, Thoughtful, Checked, Understood**, each worth **25 points**. The four scores add to a milestone grade out of 100.

| Criterion | Look for this in the submitted work |
| --- | --- |
| **Complete** | The required pieces are present and useful. |
| **Thoughtful** | The audience, alternatives, and choice have specific reasons. |
| **Checked** | Sources or behavior were checked, and the record shows what happened. |
| **Understood** | The explanation connects the need, implementation, AI assistance, and limits. |

The assessment next to each submission names evidence, explains the score, and gives a specific next revision. **A small project can earn full credit.** More features or more pages do not automatically earn more points.

## Read the brand choices as evidence

Look for a source observation connected to the chosen niche, a promise visible in actual copy/design, and two honestly explained persuasion elements. The examples link an original logo and a live style guide. Their A/B comparisons change both visual treatment and hierarchy, so they do not establish which caused a preference.

The same four criteria apply: **Complete** covers the pieces, **Thoughtful** covers reasons, **Checked** covers source/claim and user-task evidence, and **Understood** covers the student's own explanation and limits. Brand-specific feedback accompanies each example. We do not award points for liking a particular style, and earlier Brand Studio grading remains unchanged.

## Why points are lost

Every row below 25 names the gap and the points deducted. A complete submission can earn 25 for **Complete** while losing points for an unsupported choice or an incomplete check. A more ambitious project does not earn extra credit just for being larger.

For example, recording that a peer found the size guide does not show whether they completed the whole checkout task. That gap belongs under **Checked**. A full-credit milestone may still include an **optional next step**; it is not a hidden requirement.

These fictional scores were recalibrated to match the submitted evidence. The four criteria and their equal shares stay the same.

## Stronger versus developing

A stronger comparison says what the visitor attempted in both versions and uses that observation to explain the choice. A developing comparison may say only “my classmate liked B.” Both can have working websites; the evidence and reasoning differ.

A stronger acceptance check says “submitting without a topic does not save a request.” A developing check says “the form should be clear.” Feedback should help the student improve that statement, not tell them to build a larger product.

## How the sample assessments work

- **People and peer sessions are fictional.** Completed peer records are worked teaching scenarios, not real interviews.
- **The products are runnable.** A separate [technical receipt](/examples/submissions/technical-checks) lists checks actually performed on the class code. Those checks do not establish a fictional student's personal understanding.
- **Written understanding is modeled.** In a real class review, the instructor also asks the student to explain the work. These examples do not pretend that conversation happened.
- **Portfolio accounts are simulated.** Completed local résumé/GitHub/LinkedIn examples stand in for the fictional people's accounts. Real students must link their own actual profiles.
- **Portfolio Research is career exploration.** The summary uses Maya's 100/100 and Jordan's 80/100 career examples. Reference-site research inside their packets is optional practice; its separate exercise grade is not another Section 007 checkpoint. Packet averages that include that exercise are not Section 007 portfolio averages.
- **Project scores are illustrative.** We average four milestone scores equally to compare these examples. That does not create a new Canvas/course weighting, letter-grade policy, or actual student grade.

## Use the feedback

Read one submission before opening its score. Identify a strength and one gap yourself. Then compare your reasoning with the assessment. Try rewriting the weak acceptance check, comparison note, or AI-use explanation using the instructor's feedback.
