## A short Research submission

**Fictional teaching example, condensed from [Campus Cotton](/examples/submissions/campus-cotton#_1-research).** The observations come from that existing November 2 example; brand readings and personas are hypotheses. Check the sites yourself. This is a format to learn from, not a claim of new field research or peer review.

> **Idea:** Campus Cotton, one everyday white tee for student commuters.
>
> | Reference | What I noticed → my decision |
> | --- | --- |
> | [Fresh Clean Tees](https://freshcleantees.com/products/white-crew-neck) | Fit/length choices, photo-led layout, approachable Everyperson voice, displayed reviews → put measurements near size. |
> | [True Classic](https://www.trueclassictees.com/products/white-crew-neck-tee) | Several purchase paths, bold comparisons, Everyperson/Hero reading, displayed ratings → make our single-item total simple. |
> | [Buck Mason](https://www.buckmason.com/products/white-slub-curved-hem-tee) | Restrained presentation, craft details, Creator reading → use specific facts rather than “premium.” |
>
> **Two positions:** affordable everyday essential, or a blank starting point for creative outfits.
>
> **Persona A:** a commuter needs size, price, and a quick purchase; worries about fit. **Persona B:** a student styling outfits needs inspiration and product facts; worries about a generic look. Both are untested hypotheses based on the page comparison.
>
> **Choice:** A. Keep the voice plain and welcoming; use aligned type and a clear hierarchy. Show useful fit guidance and truthful product details. Reviews suggest social proof, but I cannot verify competitors' claims. A sale does not prove scarcity.
>
> **Next:** plan a visible fit guide and total. The research supports design choices; it does not prove demand.

## What is inside each submission?

- T-shirt/group: completed competitive research and two persona cards. Portfolio: use the [career reflection examples](/examples/career-reflections) for required Research; reference-site research in the packets is optional practice.
- Two written website specifications, user stories, feature priorities, and checks.
- Two working versions and a recorded launch decision.
- A completed peer-feedback, change, and retest scenario.
- QA results, an AI-use explanation, a case study, and final links.
- A score out of 100 for each milestone, criterion-by-criterion reasons, and a specific next revision.

The group projects also include work plans and individual contribution statements. The portfolios include completed fictional résumé, GitHub, and LinkedIn content.

**These are fictional students and illustrative grades.** Peer records are worked scenarios; they are not real interviews. The products are actual runnable code, with a separate [technical-check receipt](/examples/submissions/technical-checks). Real students must submit their own decisions and actual peer feedback.

## Learn from the difference

A working site is one part of the submission. Compare how the examples explain their choices and record their checks. A short, specific explanation is more useful than a longer document full of general claims.
