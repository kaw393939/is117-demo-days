# Example résumé: turn IS117 work into evidence

You can begin a credible project-focused résumé before you have a technical internship. Use completed coursework, real projects, and transferable experience to show what you can do now and where you want to grow.

This example assumes a student has **completed and verified** the listed work. The business names, group scenario, and bullets are illustrative. Replace every bracketed field and rewrite or remove any claim you cannot demonstrate. This is not a certificate of FDE readiness.

**Two connected student examples:** [Maya’s résumé](/samples/maya-chen/resume.txt) · [Jordan’s résumé](/samples/jordan-rivera/resume.txt).

## A course-based résumé example

The content below is designed to be adapted into a concise, approximately one-page résumé. Its plain structure can be copied into a document editor and exported as a selectable-text PDF.

## Complete the résumé in five steps

1. **Collect evidence.** Open your live sites, repositories, specification, work plan, and QA record. List what you actually completed.
2. **Choose a target.** Read a real [local internship description](/career/internships). Select relevant evidence and identify skills you still need to learn.
3. **Write precise bullets.** Use action + implemented scope + evidence/result. Say what you did and how someone can verify it.
4. **Check ownership and truth.** Distinguish your work from the team's work. Remove tools, metrics, and completion claims you cannot support. Label in-progress projects clearly.
5. **Format and review.** Keep headings clear, links clickable, text readable, and dates consistent. Ask a peer to inspect the document and its links. Export a PDF with selectable text and verify the exported file.

A résumé is selective. If this content does not fit one readable page, shorten the summary, combine overlapping skills, or feature the two strongest projects. A current early-career student does not need to fill space with invented experience.

## Improve the wording with evidence

| Vague or overstated claim | More useful, if accurate |
| --- | --- |
| “Expert FDE and AI engineer” | “NJIT student interested in customer-facing software and applied AI” |
| “Built a website using AI” | “Used [tool] to draft form logic, then revised validation and checked missing-input behavior” |
| “Built the entire group application” | “Implemented request intake and validated its integration with my partner's review queue” |
| “Improved sales by 40%” without measurements | “Created a simulated purchase journey and verified cart totals for [actual scenarios]” |
| “Tested everything” | “Checked [specific paths] and documented [actual limitations]” |

Do not claim course examples as your own shipped features. Likewise, using an AI API, writing automated tests, or deploying a database belongs on your résumé only if you actually did it and can explain it.

## Keep the four surfaces consistent

Your **portfolio** gives the detailed story; **GitHub** supplies the technical evidence; **LinkedIn** introduces your interests and work; your **résumé** selects the strongest relevant facts for one opportunity. Names, dates, links, technologies, and contribution claims should agree.
