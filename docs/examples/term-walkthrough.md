## Instructor findings: keep the work small

| Risk or confusion | Clarification or next step |
| --- | --- |
| “Three projects means three new reports every week.” | Reuse one process record per project. Canvas takes links and a short update; the team submits once. |
| “Two versions means six separate codebases.” | A/B can share content, data, and behavior. Compare one meaningful presentation decision. |
| Portfolio starts Nov 12 but a plan is due Nov 17. | Reuse the career reflection and business-project evidence. Short notes and simple page sketches are enough for the planning comparison; keep the required stories/checks specific. |
| Feedback becomes paperwork. | Use concise issues with observation → response/change → retest. Continue an unresolved issue across stages rather than copying it. |
| SQLite works locally but documentation links fail there. | The downloaded server serves products only. Keep the published guide open separately, as the setup guide explains. |
| Build week has three projects, A/B, and a backend. | Protect class work time and use small workflows. Do not add payments, accounts, live scheduling, or an AI product feature just to look advanced. |
| “The AI finished quickly, so a freshman can too.” | No novice timing study was run. Ask students for actual time/blockers at Nov 5 and Nov 12; adjust support or scope if evidence shows overload. |

**Readiness judgment:** the sequence is coherent, but workload is not yet validated with real students. Canvas is not ready to publish until its remaining descriptions, team settings/membership, and student submission flow are verified. The role-play cannot remove that blocker.
