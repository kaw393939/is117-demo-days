# Your group business

With a partner, build a website for a realistic business product or service. It could accept an appointment request, explain a service and collect an inquiry, register customers for an event, or support another useful task.

**Examples:** [Room Ready](/examples/submissions/room-ready) · [Study Slot](/examples/submissions/study-slot). [More ideas](/projects/ideas).

## Keep the scope small

Pick **one complete customer workflow**. For example: let someone choose a repair service, send a request, and see that it is pending. Do not add payments or an admin dashboard unless they are part of your agreed scope.

Start after the two solo projects have taught you the process. Share one project record. Both versions can use the same code and data; change one choice that matters to the customer. Each person owns a meaningful piece of the build and reviews another person's work. This is the same group project, not extra work.

[Try this flow in the Room Ready example](/examples/submissions/room-ready#_3-build-and-compare).

Choose a customer, a business need, and one complete task. “A visitor submits a request, sees a receipt, and the business can find the request” is enough to build around.

Use AI to help plan and code. An AI feature inside the product is optional. Choose tools you can explain and run.

## Practice with a client

Use an [AI-simulated customer brief](/process/simulated-customer) if it helps your team ask discovery questions and compare two proposals. Two starter `AGENTS.md` files are available. Keep the decisions in your existing project document. A real peer still performs the final walkthrough.

## Give the offer a clear identity

Choose one customer situation and explain why your service fits it. Use the same short [brand strategy process](/process/find-your-angle): a promise, brand character/voice, two visual approaches, and two honest reasons to act. For example, helpful request instructions can show a caring voice. A calm-looking page cannot prove a service is safe or qualified. Keep the brief in your shared record.

## Share the work fairly

Teams have two students, with one trio if a section has an odd number. **Every member contributes to design, development, and testing.** Agree on tasks with comparable effort and review each other's work.

| Each person should… | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Design something useful | Plan the customer form or the request summary. |
| Build a meaningful part | Implement form validation or request storage and review. |
| Test a partner's work | Check missing inputs, the main task, or the phone layout. |
| Explain their contribution | Link tasks, changes, and reviews in the shared record. |

Do not give one person all coding and the other only slides or proofreading. Adjust the work plan when a task takes longer than expected. [See a work-plan example](/process/collaboration).

## Follow the four steps together

Research three relevant sites, two possible positions, and two short persona hypotheses; choose one audience. Compare two website plans, build two working versions, and choose one. For **Group Improve**, use a real peer and follow your section's Improve assignment: **005 requires a reviewer from outside the team**; **007 requires real peer review**. Record at least one meaningful feedback-driven change and retest it. Teammate cross-review is also required in both sections.

## Check your workflow together

Give a peer the same customer task in A and B. Record where they got stuck and why you chose one version. In Improve, link what changed and what happened when they tried again. In the shared record, put each person’s name next to the work they built and the work they reviewed. [See an example](/examples/submissions/room-ready#_4-get-feedback-and-improve).

## What you submit

- Selected site, alternate version, shared repository, and setup steps.
- A [short process document](/templates/four-milestones), including research, stories, plan, feedback, changes, checks, and AI use.
- The work plan and a brief contribution statement from each member.

Each person adds a case study to their own portfolio. Both partners explain their work during the team demo. Keep final links in the shared review record; the separate final assessment reuses them and Demo Day evidence rather than asking for another report.
