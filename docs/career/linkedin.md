# Get your LinkedIn ready

LinkedIn helps someone connect your interests to work you can show. **Your portfolio must link to your actual LinkedIn profile.** A text draft is useful while planning, but the final link needs to open your profile. A free account is enough.

## Minimum checklist

- [ ] My headline says I am a student and names my interests or next opportunity.
- [ ] My About section introduces me and gives a specific project example.
- [ ] Education shows my actual NJIT program and expected graduation date.
- [ ] My T-shirt and group projects have links and explain my contribution.
- [ ] Skills reflect work I can explain; my portfolio and GitHub are easy to find.

Use the available profile sections and link fields; menu names can change. Label these businesses **course projects**. Describe completed work accurately and label work in progress. “Interested in forward deployed engineering” is a goal; it does not mean you have held that job.

## Use it effectively

Follow a few companies or people connected to your career research. Read what they build and the skills their roles require. After meeting someone at an NJIT or local event, introduce yourself with the shared context and one relevant question. A useful project link gives you something concrete to discuss.

Keep project names, dates, skills, and contributions consistent across LinkedIn, your résumé, GitHub, and portfolio. Use examples of actual work instead of unsupported claims about sales, expertise, or hiring readiness.

**Ready check:** have a classmate open the portfolio's LinkedIn link and find your student status, a project, and your contribution. Also check what a signed-out visitor can see; LinkedIn may request sign-in. The link should still lead to your own profile, not an editor, search result, or draft file.
