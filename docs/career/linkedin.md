# Get your LinkedIn ready

Explain who you are, what you are learning, and what work you can show. You do not need a technical job already to describe a useful course project.

## Complete these parts

| Part | What to include |
| --- | --- |
| Headline | Your student status, interests, and the opportunity you want. |
| About | A short introduction in your own voice, with specific project examples. |
| Education | NJIT, your actual degree, and expected graduation date. |
| Projects | The T-shirt store and group business, links, and your contribution. |
| Skills | Skills you have practiced and can explain. |
| Links | Your portfolio and GitHub through the available profile/contact fields. |

[LinkedIn introduction editor](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a547248) · [Add profile sections](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a540837/adding-sections-to-your-profile). Menu names may vary. A paid subscription is not required for this course.

## A headline to adapt

> NJIT [Major] Student | Web Development & AI-Assisted Projects | Seeking [Internship Type] Opportunities

“Interested in forward deployed engineering” can describe your goal. Use your actual current role, rather than listing a job you have not held.

## A short About section

> I’m an NJIT student interested in building useful software for business problems. In IS117, I’m practicing research, website planning, AI-assisted coding, and peer review.
>
> My projects include a simulated T-shirt store and a team website for [service]. I personally built [feature] and reviewed [partner’s work]. My portfolio shows the choices, feedback, and checks behind the projects.
>
> I’m interested in [opportunity] and continuing to learn [next skill]. Explore my work at [portfolio link].

Replace the brackets and use your own wording. Label class businesses as **course projects**. Explain your own contribution separately from the team’s result. Use present tense for work in progress and past tense for completed work.

## Check the whole story

A useful project description can connect a researched audience need to one design choice and its check. Use actual evidence, not a claim that your design increased sales.

Your LinkedIn, GitHub, résumé, and portfolio should agree about project names, dates, skills, and contributions. Open the links as a visitor and fix anything broken.
