# Build a professional LinkedIn profile

LinkedIn introduces you to people who may never inspect your code. Explain who you are, what you are learning, the work you can demonstrate, and what opportunities interest you. Link that introduction to your portfolio and GitHub evidence.

## Build the profile around your current stage

| Element | What to communicate |
| --- | --- |
| Introduction | Recognizable name, accurate education/location, and a specific headline |
| About | Interests, relevant projects, current skills, and career direction in your own voice |
| Education | NJIT, your actual degree/major, expected graduation, and relevant coursework |
| Projects | The individual and group work, with your contribution and links |
| Skills | Skills you have practiced and can explain; connect them to evidence |
| Links | Portfolio and GitHub through the available contact/project fields |

Choose a clear, appropriate profile image and background if you use them; paid photography or a subscription is not needed for this coursework. Use a professional contact route without exposing personal details you do not want public.

LinkedIn's introduction editor includes headline, education, location, and contact information. Menu names and available sections can vary. [Edit your introduction](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a547248) · [Add profile sections](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a540837/adding-sections-to-your-profile).

## Example headline

> NJIT [Major] Student | Web Development & AI-Assisted Projects | Seeking [Internship Type] Opportunities

Replace the brackets. Choose a direction such as web development, software engineering, or applied AI. “Interested in forward deployed engineering” can express a goal; do not present an aspirational role as a job you already hold.

## Example About section to adapt

> I'm a [major] student at NJIT interested in building useful software for business problems. Through IS117, I'm developing experience in web design, competitive research, specification-driven development, and AI-assisted coding.
>
> My projects include an individual Plain White T-Shirt business website and a partner project for [business/service]. On the team project, I contributed [specific design work], built [specific feature], and tested [specific workflow developed by my partner]. My portfolio explains the research, decisions, and verification behind the work.
>
> I'm seeking [type of opportunity] in the NYC/Newark area and continuing to develop [next skills]. You can explore my work at [portfolio URL] and [GitHub URL].

Use present tense for work in progress and past tense for completed work. Include only what you have actually done. Your own voice and specific examples are more persuasive than a list of fashionable terms.

## Example project description

**Plain White T-Shirt Company — IS117 course project**

> Developed a simulated eCommerce concept for [audience]. Compared [actual number] competing brands and translated findings into [specific feature/design decisions]. Implemented [working features] using [actual technologies] and checked [actual QA scenarios]. [Live link] · [Repository link]

For the group project, identify the business goal and your contribution separately from the team's overall result. Label classroom projects as projects; do not list a fictional class business as an employer that hired you.

## Make the work accessible

Include your portfolio URL and GitHub profile in the available link/contact fields. Add project links where your account supports them. If a Featured option is available, it can highlight selected work, but it is not required. LinkedIn documents some profile-content featuring options as Premium-only; use the ordinary profile text and links rather than purchasing a plan for the course. [LinkedIn's current profile-content Featured guidance](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1586667).

## Build a professional network thoughtfully

Connect with classmates and people you actually know. If you contact someone about their work, refer to a specific interest or question and keep the note brief. Share a project when you can explain a meaningful decision, result, or lesson. Follow relevant employers to learn about their work and openings. Bulk connection requests and generic praise do little to explain your interests.

## Before Demo Day

Check that your headline, About section, résumé, and portfolio tell a consistent story. Verify your links. Make sure group contributions and skill claims agree across all four places. Review how your profile appears to other viewers using LinkedIn's available visibility controls.
