# Make a résumé you can explain

**Your portfolio must include a readable résumé.** Link a web version or a downloadable file clearly. For a first résumé, aim for about one readable page; a selectable-text PDF is a useful download for applications. You do not need a technical job already to describe course projects.

## Minimum checklist

- [ ] My name, professional contact, portfolio, GitHub, and LinkedIn links are correct.
- [ ] Education shows my actual degree and expected graduation date.
- [ ] Skills describe things I can demonstrate.
- [ ] Project bullets identify my contribution and a specific result or check.
- [ ] Course projects and work in progress are labeled; no experience or metrics are invented.
- [ ] The exported résumé opens, is readable, and has working links.

## Build yours in four steps

1. **Choose a target.** Use a role from your [career research](/career/explore). Pick relevant evidence and note the skills you still need.
2. **Write from your work.** Open your code, specification, and review record. Use action + what you did + evidence: “Implemented size validation and checked empty, valid, and unsupported inputs.”
3. **Make it readable.** Use clear headings and simple formatting. Shorten repeated claims before shrinking the text. Keep your public contact details professional.
4. **Export and check.** Open the final file, select its text, click the links, and check it on a phone. Ask a peer whether your own group contribution is clear.

## Use it effectively

Adjust the order and emphasis for each opportunity while keeping the facts consistent. “Interested in FDE work” describes a direction; completing IS117 does not establish readiness for every FDE internship. Be ready to show and explain every project bullet, including how you used and checked AI output.

**Ready check:** can a reviewer find your education, two projects, your contribution, and a working portfolio link quickly? Does every claim match the current project? Update “planned” work only after completing it.
