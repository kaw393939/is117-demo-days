# Portfolio Research: explore two roles

**Portfolio Research is due Thursday November 12, 2026:** **005 at 11:30 a.m.; 007 at 8:30 a.m. Eastern.** Introduce November 5; discuss/work November 9 in 005 or November 10 in 007. Use draft findings to begin Plan, review it November 12, and submit Plan November 16 in 005 or November 17 in 007. [005 class plan](/course/section-005) · [007 class plan](/course/section-007). Use your matching Canvas assignment.

You do not have to decide your career now. Use real descriptions to find work that interests you, see what you can already demonstrate, and choose something to learn next.

**Your output:** two short role cards and about **250–350 words of reflection** submitted privately in Canvas. Bullets are fine. This is the Research checkpoint for your individual portfolio, not a fourth project or an extra research report. Your Canvas assignment gives the section-specific deadline.

Use the findings to choose your portfolio reader, goals, and evidence. Keep the full reflection private in Canvas; carry suitable professional goals into your portfolio record. Portfolio references are optional inspiration, not another competitive-landscape submission.

## 1. Understand the work · about 5 minutes

Read [What is an FDE?](/career/fde). A forward deployed engineer learns a customer's problem, builds useful software, and helps people use it. Look at [job examples and pay](/career/jobs-and-pay) for concrete skills. IS117 starts the process; it does not make you ready for every role.

## 2. Compare two descriptions · about 15–20 minutes

Start with [NYC-area internships](/career/internships), then **open the original employer descriptions**. Compare two FDE roles, or one FDE role and a related software, implementation, or applied-AI role. You may use a relevant role elsewhere if that fits your goals.

For each, make a small card:

| Record | What to write |
| --- | --- |
| Source | Employer, exact title, original URL, and date you checked it |
| Where and when | Location, work arrangement, and program dates if stated |
| Eligibility | What the posting publicly requires: student year/degree or other qualifications |
| Actual work | Three skills or tasks, explained in your own words |
| Deadline and status | Exact deadline wording, or **not stated**; current, closed, or not verified |

A course list is a starting point, not a live application system. If a page has closed, label it as a historical learning example or choose another source. Do not infer a deadline from an annual recruiting pattern. Application deadlines are different from your Canvas due date. Pay can be hourly or annualized; it is not automatically total internship earnings.

## 3. Discuss your findings in class · about 15 minutes

Bring your two cards to the feedback meeting. Tell a classmate one task that interests you and one requirement you do not yet meet. Ask what project evidence would make your claim believable. Keep the rest of class-work time for the current project milestone.

Look at **one** [completed career reflection](/examples/career-reflections), then its feedback. You do not need to read every career article or copy an example's goals.

## 4. Write your reflection · about 15 minutes

Answer these four prompts in your own voice:

1. **Interest:** What attracts you, does not fit, or remains uncertain? Use details from both descriptions. Preferring another career is a valid conclusion.
2. **Evidence:** Connect two role expectations to your actual IS117 work. Link an artifact if available. Label unfinished work as planned; do not claim an example as your own.
3. **Gaps:** Name two skills or experiences to develop beyond this course. [Use the skills guide](/career/skills) if a job term is unfamiliar.
4. **Next step:** Choose one action you can finish in about a week. State what you will produce and how you will check it. [Later courses](/career/course-pathway) can support a longer plan, but “learn AI” is not a specific next action.

Paste both cards and the reflection into the Canvas assignment. The cards do not count toward the suggested word length. No job application, employer message, paid account, public reflection, or disclosure of private eligibility circumstances is required.

## What earns credit

| Criterion | Share | What matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Complete | 25% | Two role cards, sources, and a reasonable reflection |
| Thoughtful | 25% | Specific comparison and honest reasons, including uncertainty |
| Checked | 25% | Original sources, dates, eligibility, deadlines, and limits checked |
| Understood | 25% | Your evidence, two gaps, and a concrete next step explained |

We grade the process, not career certainty, salary ambitions, agreement with the instructor, or present eligibility. AI can help explain terms or organize notes; verify facts and briefly say how you used it. Keep your reflection in your own voice.
