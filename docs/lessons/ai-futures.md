# AI futures: read a prediction without treating it as fact

**Why it matters:** AI tools and job descriptions change. You need to be able to tell what someone has shown, what they expect, and what decision you can make now.

## Read

- [Sam Altman, *Moore’s Law for Everything*](https://moores.samaltman.com/): an essay about how AI could change the cost of work and how society might distribute the gains.
- [*AI 2027*](https://ai-2027.com/): read the main scenario and both endings. These are scenarios built on assumptions, not a record of events or a timetable to take for granted.

## Try it

For each reading, write one **claim**, the **assumption** it depends on, and what **evidence** would make you more or less confident. Then compare who benefits, who bears a risk, and how each author expects work to change. You don't have to agree with either one.

Bring it back to web development. Suppose an AI assistant writes a checkout form in minutes. What work still belongs to you before a customer can use it? Think about the spec, the test, and the decision about what gets shipped. End with two questions you’d actually want to discuss.

## Check your work

If a sentence says “will,” can you say whose prediction it is and why? Cite the reading near the claim. Explain one concrete effect on your own learning or career instead of promising that a job will disappear or appear.
