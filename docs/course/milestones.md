# Your assignment: four steps

Build a **[T-shirt store](/projects/white-tshirt)**, a **[group business site](/projects/group-project)**, and your **[personal portfolio](/projects/portfolio)**. Practice a professional process using AI to solve a realistic business problem. You do not need to be an expert.

**Start with your next class:** [005](/course/section-005#before-next-class) · [007](/course/section-007#before-next-class). Open one matching example, then update [the short template](/templates/four-milestones). Work on the current step. Detailed templates and career articles are optional reading; the résumé, actual GitHub/LinkedIn profiles, and all three project repository links are required in your [portfolio ready checklist](/projects/portfolio#portfolio-ready-checklist). [See the visual roadmap](/course/overview#how-to-manage-the-work).

## What to submit at each step

Update [the same record](/templates/four-milestones) for each project. For your T-shirt and portfolio projects, start with the [Project Plan Guide](/process/project-plan/). Use that plan during Build and update it as you work.

| Step | What to do and show | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Research** | For T-shirt or group, compare three relevant sites, two positions, and two audience hypotheses. Link a dated source, say what you saw, and show which decision it informed. For Portfolio Research, submit the role cards and reflection privately. | [Short research example](/examples/submissions/#a-short-research-submission) · [Career activity](/career/explore) |
| **2. Plan** | Compare two approaches. Write three to five stories and the checks that would show they work. For group work, name who will build and review each part. | [Campus Cotton’s Plan](/examples/submissions/campus-cotton#_2-plan) |
| **3. Build** | Publish both versions on GitHub Pages and link them with your source. Run the same task in both, record what happened, and explain your provisional choice. For the T-shirt store, show the same saved demo order after reloading your GitHub Pages site. | [Compare A and B](/examples/submissions/campus-cotton#_3-build-and-compare) · [Save and reload check](/examples/submissions/run-examples#run-the-save-and-reload-check) |
| **4. Improve** | Watch a peer use the selected version, change something meaningful, and have them retry. Link the feedback, change, retest, and selected site. | [Room Ready’s feedback and retest](/examples/submissions/room-ready#_4-get-feedback-and-improve) |

Carry forward the earlier Brand Studio decisions in your existing record: audience promise, brand voice, logo/style guide, visual alternatives, and two truthful persuasion applications. [Find your brand’s angle](/process/find-your-angle). This adds no new report or set of websites.

A **persona** describes a user and their needs. A **user story** explains what that user wants to do and why. A **specification**, or **spec**, is your website plan. [See how they connect](/process/research-to-features).

## Reviews and final project grades

The practice reviews are about what you tried and learned. Final assessments use the finished work and your Demo Day explanation.

Each final assessment reuses the finished site, existing code and process links, your actual contribution, and your explanation. A **No submission** row is not a new upload or an earlier hand-in. Use Canvas for deadlines, points, category weights, and feedback.

## How each step is graded

| Criterion | Share | What we look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Complete** | 25% | A reasonable attempt at the required work, with links that open. |
| **Thoughtful** | 25% | Useful alternatives; connect evidence to your audience, brand choices, and features. |
| **Checked** | 25% | You checked sources, claims, or behavior. The final step includes real peer feedback on understanding and use, a change, and a retest. |
| **Understood** | 25% | You can explain your work, AI use, contribution, and limits. |

### What counts as a check?

- **Research:** Open the original source and make sure it supports the observation you wrote down.
- **Plan:** Give a classmate a task and tell them what they should be able to see or do. Mark it as planned until you run it.
- **Build:** Record the steps and actual result. A failed check is useful evidence when you show what happened and what you’ll try next.
- **Improve:** Have a peer retry after the change. Record what they did and whether the change helped.

Be ready to open one piece of evidence, explain the choice it changed, and say what could make you change your mind. For a team project, be ready to show the work you did and what you reviewed. Earlier assignments use the original criteria. Check Canvas for category weights, scores, and feedback.
