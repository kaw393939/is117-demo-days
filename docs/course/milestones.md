# Four milestones. Three projects. One process.

**Practice a professional process using AI to address a realistic business situation.** You are being introduced to the work; you do not need to be an expert.

<LearningDiagram kind="process" />

Apply the process to your **individual T-shirt store**, **group business**, and **individual portfolio**. Keep one short, evolving document per project. The portfolio can use a smaller comparison of reference sites and reviewer audiences.

| Milestone | What you submit |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Research** | Compare three relevant sites; consider two positions and two persona hypotheses; choose a primary audience and explain why. Link sources and identify assumptions. |
| **2. Plan** | Two concise website approaches, three to five user stories, required features, acceptance checks, and deferred work. Group members divide comparable design, development, and QA work. |
| **3. Build** | Two working versions, a meaningful comparison, and a proposed launch choice. Versions may share code and the core workflow. |
| **4. Review and launch** | A real peer walkthrough of each project, at least one meaningful improvement per project, a retest, and the chosen launch version. Include QA, AI use, contributions, and limitations. |

## The same simple rubric each time

Each milestone uses four equally weighted criteria. Any gradebook points and category weights will be recorded in Canvas; these proportions do not change the syllabus’s course weights.

| Criterion | Share | What meets introductory expectations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Complete** | 25% | A reasonable, relevant attempt at the items for this milestone, with accessible evidence. |
| **Thoughtful** | 25% | Credible alternatives, a clear audience/business need, and a choice supported by specific reasons. |
| **Checked** | 25% | Sources, behavior, or implementation checked appropriately for the stage. In the last milestone, actual peer feedback leads to a documented change and retest. |
| **Understood** | 25% | You can explain the work, AI assistance, limitations, and your own contribution. |

Use **meets expectations**, **developing**, or **missing** to discuss each criterion. An identified limitation is acceptable; describing unfinished or untested work as complete is not evidence of understanding. Group members share the product, but each must demonstrate meaningful personal work and cross-review.

## Work together during class

**Attendance is required.** Class meetings include scheduled time to work with partners, receive feedback, and improve projects. Bring your computer, current work, and a specific task or question for a peer. Give feedback as well as receive it.

A partner or another classmate may review an individual site. For the group product, a reviewer outside the team can offer a fresh perspective. Agree on a realistic task, observe before explaining, record what happened, make a meaningful change, and check it again. Arrange absence-related needs or accommodations privately through Canvas under course/university policies; no new attendance penalty is established by this page.

## Keep the final submission small

For each project, provide the selected live version, comparison version, repository, and short process document. The portfolio also links your résumé, GitHub, LinkedIn, and both project case studies. Group members each include their contribution statement. Use Canvas for submission and grades; use GitHub issues to request a Demo Day slot.
