# Your assignment: four steps

Build a **[T-shirt store](/projects/white-tshirt)**, a **[group business site](/projects/group-project)**, and your **[personal portfolio](/projects/portfolio)**. Practice a professional process using AI to solve a realistic business problem. You do not need to be an expert.

Copy [the short template](/templates/four-milestones) and check [your dates](/course/schedule). Work on the current step. Detailed templates and career articles are optional reading; the résumé and actual GitHub/LinkedIn links are still required in your portfolio. [See the visual roadmap](/course/overview#how-to-manage-the-work).

## What to submit at each step

Use [one short document per project](/templates/four-milestones) and update it as you go. For the portfolio, career exploration counts as Research; use its findings for your Plan.

| Step | Submit this |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Research** | T-shirt/group: compare three relevant sites, two positions, and two personas; choose an audience. Portfolio: complete career role cards and reflection, then identify your reader and evidence. |
| **2. Plan** | Compare two short website plans. Write three to five user stories, essential features, and checks for success. For the group project, divide work fairly. |
| **3. Build** | Link two working versions and explain which you plan to launch. Share code where useful; make the difference meaningful. |
| **4. Improve** | Have a peer walk through each project. Make at least one meaningful change per project, retest, and publish your selected versions. |

Carry forward the earlier Brand Studio decisions in your existing record: audience promise, brand voice, logo/style guide, visual alternatives, and two truthful persuasion applications. [Find your brand’s angle](/process/find-your-angle). This adds no new report or set of websites.

A **persona** describes a user and their needs. A **user story** explains what that user wants to do and why. A **specification**, or **spec**, is your website plan. [See how they connect](/process/research-to-features).

## How to submit

**One submission per active project each week.** Keep three evolving records: your T-shirt record, your portfolio record, and the team’s shared business record. Update the current stage; do not make a new report each week.

For **section 007**, T-shirt Research is due November 10. Career exploration is portfolio Research, due November 12; portfolio Plan is due November 17. The group forms first, then runs about a week behind: Research November 17, Plan November 24, Build December 3, Improve December 6 at 11:59 p.m. [Follow every class and deadline](/course/section-007).

Submit each project in its own Canvas box: record link, current site/repository links, [feedback issue links](/process/github-feedback), and a short update. **For portfolio Research, paste the two role cards and reflection directly into the private Canvas text box instead.** The group submits once and names contributions. Each project has four checkpoints; the career reflection adds no extra research report.

**Canvas setup is being aligned one section at a time.** Section 007 has separate unpublished project placeholders. Section 005’s earlier combined individual-review drafts have not yet been converted; follow its published Canvas directions. No new draft is an active submission box until published.

In 007, T-shirt/portfolio final review records are due December 3 at the start of class; the group Build is due then, with group Improve due December 6 at 11:59 p.m. Continue fixes and rehearsal on the same sites and records at the same URLs; all final work must be ready December 6 at 11:59 p.m. No extra individual Canvas assignment is added. Send a corrected URL through Canvas Inbox if a submitted link changes. A demo-signup issue does not submit coursework.

## How each step is graded

| Criterion | Share | What we look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Complete** | 25% | A reasonable attempt at the required work, with links that open. |
| **Thoughtful** | 25% | Useful alternatives; connect evidence to your audience, brand choices, and features. |
| **Checked** | 25% | You checked sources, claims, or behavior. The final step includes real peer feedback on understanding and use, a change, and a retest. |
| **Understood** | 25% | You can explain your work, AI use, contribution, and limits. |

A small, well-explained attempt can earn full credit. We assess your reasons and checks, not a preferred aesthetic or professional market validation. Earlier assignments retain their original criteria.

These are the rubric shares for each milestone, not changes to the syllabus’s course-grade weights. Canvas will hold gradebook points and feedback. Each group member must show meaningful personal work and review a teammate’s work.

[See completed submissions with grades and feedback](/examples/submissions/grading-guide). These illustrate how the rubric applies; they are not real student marks.
