The last two regular meetings of each section are Demo Days. Each team has one slot; **every student** shows their own portfolio and T-shirt site and explains their role in the group project.

## Presentation format

| Portion | Pair | Trio | What to show |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Each student's individual work | 2 minutes each = 4 | 2 minutes each = 6 | Portfolio, career/brand direction, T-shirt site and one research/spec decision; identify your group contribution |
| Shared business demonstration | 4 minutes | 4 minutes | Audience/problem, working journey, spec, balanced work division, AI use and QA evidence |
| Questions and transition | 2 minutes | 2 minutes | Answer clearly and hand over on time |
| **Total** | **10 minutes** | **12 minutes** | The next team is ready before the slot begins |

Show the product operating. Slides may help, but should not replace a working demonstration. Explain one trade-off and one change made after feedback. Use the repository/spec/QA record as supporting evidence rather than reading every document aloud.

## Sign up through GitHub Issues

1. Agree on a team, project title, section, and preferred available slot. There is one reserved trio slot per section.
2. Open the section-specific **Demo signup** form using the schedule's request link, or [choose an issue form](https://github.com/kaw393939/is117-demo-days/issues/new/choose).
3. Submit **one issue per team**. Include GitHub handles, project links, each member's portfolio/T-shirt links, and your presentation plan. In-progress links are allowed; update them before your demo.
4. A request is **not a booking**. The instructor resolves conflicts, comments with confirmation, and updates the published schedule. Earlier requests receive preference where practical; instructor adjustments may be necessary.
5. Check that your issue number and project appear in the schedule. To change a request, update the existing issue rather than submitting duplicates.

Use public-facing names or GitHub handles. Keep student IDs, grades, private circumstances, passwords, and private meeting links in Canvas. No student names or bookings are prepopulated here.

## Ready-to-present checklist

- Live portfolio, T-shirt site, group site, repositories, specification, and QA notes are ready to open.
- Your portfolio links to a readable résumé and professional GitHub/LinkedIn profiles; descriptions agree with the work you will show.
- Each member can explain their own work and the integrated result.
- Synthetic demo data and a repeatable starting state are prepared.
- The main journey and a useful validation/error state have been checked.
- A short backup recording or screenshots are ready if a live dependency fails; label them accurately.
- The team has rehearsed within its time limit.
